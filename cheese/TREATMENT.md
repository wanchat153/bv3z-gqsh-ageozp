# The Bank That Keeps Cheese — treatment (style: animation3d)

Wordless, 1080×1920, 30 fps, ~48 s. No text on screen. Original characters only.

## Facts (sources in FACTS.md)
Credem (Credito Emiliano), Emilia-Romagna, Italy, has lent against Parmigiano-Reggiano wheels since 1953. Makers need money while
wheels age 18–36 months; the bank stores the pledged wheels in climate-controlled warehouses (room for ~440,000 wheels of ~36 kg),
lends 70–80 % of their value, and sells them only if a loan isn't repaid. Experts turn and inspect the wheels. Thieves have broken in;
the last time they took 570 wheels — and didn't get far, because cheese wheels are hard to hide.

## Three structures considered
1. **Day in the vault** — follow one inspector through a shift. Clear, but no protagonist with something at stake.
2. **The heist** — open on thieves, flash back to why the cheese is there. Punchy, but the clever banking idea becomes background.
3. **One maker, one wheel** (chosen) — a cheesemaker with a perfect young wheel and no money; the bank swaps it for cash; the vault reveal; the
   wheel ages; a night break-in fails absurdly; two years later the golden wheel pays everything back. A protagonist, a scale reveal, a gag, a payoff.

## Sound
Mandolin/pizzicato tarantella (light, Italian), accordion colour; dairy: bubbling, steam, cow moo; street: footsteps, rolling wheel rumble; bank: echoing marble, hammer taps (ting-ting), stamp thunk; vault door: deep clunk + hiss + choir swell at the reveal; machine: whirr/brush; night: pizzicato sneaking, cart squeak, wheel rolling crescendo, crash; siren; final: warm accordion, cork pop, laugh-free contentment, a last bell.
